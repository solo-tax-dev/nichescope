Best Etsy Alternatives for UK Sellers: An Honest Guide

If you sell on Etsy and you're wondering whether there's somewhere better, you're not alone. Maybe fees are eating into your margins. Maybe your shop has gone quiet and you're questioning whether the platform still works for you. Maybe you just want to know what else is out there before committing further.

This guide covers the most realistic alternatives for UK-based sellers — not a list of every marketplace that exists, but a practical look at which platforms are actually worth considering depending on what you make and sell.

Why UK Sellers Look for Etsy Alternatives

The reasons vary. Some sellers are frustrated by rising fees — Etsy's transaction fee, payment processing, and offsite ads can add up quickly, especially on lower-priced handmade items. Others find that their niche has become heavily saturated on Etsy, making it harder to get visibility without paying for ads. Some simply want to reduce reliance on a single platform.

A few sellers leave after a policy change or a shop suspension. Others stay on Etsy but want to add a second sales channel to diversify their income.

None of these reasons are wrong. But it's worth being clear-eyed about what you're moving to, not just what you're moving away from.

The Main Alternatives Worth Knowing

Not On The High Street

Not On The High Street (NOTHS) is one of the most established UK-specific marketplaces for handmade, personalised, and gift products. It has a curated feel — you apply to join and not everyone is accepted. That selectivity means less competition once you're in, but it also means you can't simply open a shop today.

Their commission rates are higher than Etsy's, and there's an annual membership fee. However, the buyer demographic skews towards people looking for premium, gift-ready products and willing to pay for them. If your products photograph well and have a strong gift angle, NOTHS is worth a serious look.

Folksy

Folksy is a UK-only handmade marketplace, deliberately modelled on Etsy but focused entirely on British makers. If you make things by hand and want to sell to a UK audience, Folksy has genuine appeal. The community is smaller than Etsy, which means less traffic, but also fewer sellers competing for the same searches.

Folksy charges a listing fee per item or a flat monthly fee for unlimited listings. Transaction fees are lower than Etsy's. It tends to suit craft-led, genuinely handmade products rather than print-on-demand or resale items.

Amazon Handmade

Amazon Handmade gives sellers access to Amazon's enormous customer base. If traffic is your concern with Etsy, Amazon Handmade solves that problem — the platform has more visitors than any other marketplace.

The trade-offs are significant, though. Amazon's referral fees are higher than Etsy's transaction fees. The interface is more complex to manage. And Amazon buyers are generally conditioned to expect fast, cheap delivery — which doesn't always match what handmade sellers can offer. That said, for certain product types (especially personalised gifts and home decor), it can perform well.

eBay

eBay is not traditionally thought of as a handmade marketplace, but it remains a large platform with significant UK traffic. For sellers whose products are more craft-supply, vintage, or collectible, eBay can work well. It's less well-suited to premium handmade goods because buyer expectations around price are different.

eBay's fee structure has changed over the years. For most categories, there are no listing fees up to a certain number of listings per month, but final value fees apply on each sale. It's worth running the numbers for your specific category before assuming it's cheaper than Etsy.

Your Own Website

Many sellers eventually consider building their own shop — usually via Shopify, Squarespace, or WooCommerce. The appeal is obvious: no marketplace fees, full control over your branding, and no risk of policy changes affecting your shop.

The challenge is traffic. Marketplaces like Etsy bring buyers to you. Your own website brings nobody unless you do the work to attract them — through SEO, social media, email marketing, or paid advertising. Most sellers who build their own site find it works best as a complement to a marketplace, not a replacement, at least initially.

Depop and Vinted

These are worth a brief mention for sellers whose products include vintage or second-hand items. Depop in particular has a strong UK user base and an audience that actively seeks out vintage clothing, accessories, and one-of-a-kind pieces. If your Etsy shop includes vintage alongside handmade, Depop may be worth testing.

What to Think About Before Switching

Where Is Your Buyer?

Before moving platforms, the most useful question is: where do your customers already shop? If you sell personalised gifts, your buyer is likely already on Etsy or NOTHS. If you sell vintage clothing, Depop may reach them more easily. The best platform is the one where your specific customer is already browsing.

What Are the Real Fee Differences?

Fee comparisons between platforms can be misleading if you only look at headline rates. Etsy's transaction fee is one number, but listing fees, payment processing, and optional advertising all stack on top. The same is true elsewhere. Calculate what you'd actually keep on a typical sale across platforms before concluding one is cheaper.

What Does the Competition Look Like?

Switching to a smaller platform can mean less competition, but it also means less traffic. Less competition is only an advantage if there are still enough buyers to make it worth your time.

When researching any marketplace — whether you're evaluating Etsy or an alternative — it helps to look at what's actually visible on the page: how many listings exist for your product type, what prices competitors are charging, and how many reviews the established sellers have. Those are the signals that tell you whether a niche is viable. Don't Assume the Grass Is Greener

Every platform has its frustrations. NOTHS has high commission. Amazon Handmade has a steep learning curve. Folksy has less traffic. Your own website has no built-in audience. None of these are reasons to avoid those platforms entirely, but they're worth knowing before you invest time setting up elsewhere.

Running Multiple Platforms at Once

Many experienced UK sellers don't choose one platform — they list on two or three. Etsy plus Folksy. Etsy plus their own website. NOTHS plus Amazon Handmade. The admin overhead is real, but so is the benefit of not depending entirely on one company's algorithm or policy decisions.

If you're going to run multiple platforms, keeping your inventory and pricing consistent matters. A buyer who finds you on Etsy and then visits your website shouldn't find wildly different prices or a shop that looks abandoned.

Researching Before You Commit

Check what competitors are actually charging. Look at how many reviews top listings have. Understand whether the market is crowded or still open.

FAQ

Is Folksy better than Etsy for UK sellers?

It depends on your product and priorities. Folksy has lower fees and a UK-only focus, which some sellers prefer. But Etsy has far more traffic and a larger buyer base. Many UK sellers test both rather than choosing one exclusively.

Can I sell on multiple marketplaces at the same time?

Yes, and many sellers do. There's no rule preventing you from listing on Etsy, Folksy, and your own website simultaneously. The main consideration is keeping your stock, pricing, and branding consistent across each.

Is Not On The High Street worth applying to?

If your products are genuinely handmade or personalised and photograph well as gifts, NOTHS is worth applying to. The higher commission and membership fee make more sense for higher-priced products where the margin absorbs those costs comfortably.

Do Etsy alternatives have the same fee problems as Etsy?

Every platform has fees of some kind — listing fees, commission, payment processing, or membership charges. Some are lower than Etsy's, some are higher depending on your product category. The only way to know what you'd actually keep is to calculate it for your specific price point and volume on each platform you're considering.
