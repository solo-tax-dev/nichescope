Etsy Fees UK Calculator: What You Actually Keep Per Sale

If you're selling on Etsy from the UK, working out your actual profit is more involved than it looks. Between listing fees, transaction fees, payment processing, offsite ads, and VAT, the gap between your sale price and what lands in your bank account can be surprisingly wide. This article walks through every fee, shows you how to calculate your real margin, and explains how pricing fits into a broader picture of competition research.

Every Etsy Fee UK Sellers Need to Know

Etsy charges several distinct fees. They don't all appear in the same place, and some are easy to overlook until your payment account statement arrives.

Listing Fee

Etsy charges $0.20 USD per listing, renewed every four months or when a listing sells and auto-renews. For UK sellers this is billed in USD and converted at the rate Etsy applies on the day, so the GBP amount varies slightly. Budget roughly £0.16 per listing slot as a starting estimate, but check your own statement for the exact converted figure.

Transaction Fee

Etsy takes 6.5% of the total order amount — that means your item price plus any shipping you charge. This is one of the most significant costs per sale and applies to every transaction.

Payment Processing Fee (Etsy Payments)

If you use Etsy Payments (required in the UK for most sellers), you pay a payment processing fee on top. For UK sellers this is currently 4% plus £0.20 per transaction. So on a £20 sale you'd pay £0.80 plus £0.20 — that's £1.00 in processing alone, on top of the transaction fee.

Offsite Ads Fee

Etsy runs offsite ads on Google, Facebook, Pinterest, and similar platforms. If a buyer clicks one of those ads and purchases within 30 days, Etsy charges you 15% of the order value (or 12% if your shop has made over $10,000 in the past 365 days). Sellers under the $10,000 threshold can't opt out. This fee can catch people off guard because it's not applied at the moment of sale — it shows up later in your payment account.

UK VAT on Etsy Fees

Etsy charges UK VAT (currently 20%) on top of its own fees. So the 6.5% transaction fee effectively becomes 7.8% once VAT is added, and the payment processing fee is similarly uplifted. If you're VAT-registered as a business you can reclaim this, but most Etsy sellers are not VAT-registered, so it's a real cost.

Optional: Etsy Ads

Etsy's on-platform advertising lets you promote listings within Etsy search. You set a daily budget and pay per click. This is optional and separate from offsite ads. If you use it, factor your ad spend into your margin calculations.

A Practical UK Fees Calculation

Let's work through a straightforward example. Suppose you sell a digital print for £15 with no shipping cost.

  • Listing fee: approx £0.16
  • Transaction fee (6.5%): £0.98
  • VAT on transaction fee (20%): £0.20
  • Payment processing (4% + £0.20): £0.80
  • VAT on processing fee (20%): £0.16
  • Total fees (excluding offsite ads): approx £2.30
  • You keep: approx £12.70

If that sale came through an offsite ad, add 15% of £15 (£2.25) to your costs, leaving you with closer to £10.45 before your own production costs.

For a physical product you also need to factor in materials, packaging, your time, and any postage shortfall if you're offering free shipping or flat rates.

How to Build Your Own Etsy Fees Spreadsheet

The cleanest approach is a simple spreadsheet with columns for sale price, each fee as a formula, and a final 'net to seller' figure. Once you've built it once, you can paste in different price points and see instantly whether a product is worth making at that price.

Key columns to include:

  • Sale price (GBP)
  • Shipping charged to buyer
  • Listing fee (fixed at approx £0.16)
  • Transaction fee (sale price + shipping × 0.065)
  • VAT on transaction fee (× 0.20)
  • Payment processing fee ((sale price + shipping) × 0.04 + 0.20)
  • VAT on processing fee (× 0.20)
  • Offsite ads fee if applicable ((sale price + shipping) × 0.15)
  • Your own costs (materials, packaging, postage, time)
  • Net profit

Etsy's own fee page is the authoritative source for current rates — check it any time you're doing a serious pricing review, since rates can change.

Why Pricing and Competition Research Go Together

Working out your fees only solves half the problem. The other half is understanding what price the market will actually bear — and that means looking at what competitors are charging and how well their listings are performing.

This is where many sellers get misled. A number of popular Etsy research tools show 'estimated' monthly sales and revenue figures. These estimates are derived from third-party modelling, not data Etsy publishes, because Etsy does not make individual shop sales figures public. The estimates can be a rough guide, but they're not verified numbers and can vary widely from reality.

A more grounded approach is to look at what's actually visible on Etsy's own pages: prices, review counts, listing ages, whether a shop is running ads. These are real signals. A listing with a high review count at a certain price point tells you something real about what buyers are willing to pay. Review velocity (how quickly reviews are accumulating relative to when the listing was created) gives a rough sense of sales activity without requiring anyone to guess at a number.

Checking Competition Before You Set Your Price

Before finalising a price, search your target keyword on Etsy and look at the first two pages of results. Note:

  • Price range: What are the cheapest and most expensive comparable listings? Where does the cluster sit?
  • Review counts: Are the top listings well-established (hundreds or thousands of reviews) or is there a mix including newer shops?
  • Ad density: How many of the visible results are 'Ad' listings? High ad density can mean the organic spots are harder to win, or it can mean established sellers are heavily investing in paid placement — both are useful signals.
  • Listing age vs reviews: A listing that's been live two years with fifty reviews has a very different trajectory from one that's six months old with the same count.

Pricing Strategy Once You Know Your Costs and Competition

With your fee structure mapped out and a clear picture of the competitive landscape, you can make a more deliberate pricing decision. A few patterns worth considering:

Don't price at the bottom of the market by default. If your fees eat a significant chunk of margin, undercutting the field often means working for very little. Competing on quality, presentation, and customer experience tends to be more sustainable.

Factor in your time. Especially for made-to-order or handmade items, the hours you spend are a real cost. If your margin doesn't cover a reasonable hourly rate, it's worth either raising the price or reconsidering the product.

Test price anchoring. Etsy shoppers see multiple listings in a row. If your listing sits in a reasonable middle-price position with strong photos and good reviews, it can convert better than the cheapest option — buyers often associate low price with low quality in handmade markets.

Account for offsite ads from day one. If you're under the opt-out threshold, build a version of your margin calculation that includes the 15% offsite ads fee. If the product still makes sense at that margin, you're in a resilient position.

Frequently Asked Questions

What percentage does Etsy take in the UK?

Etsy takes 6.5% of the total order value as a transaction fee, plus a payment processing fee of 4% and £0.20 per transaction for UK sellers using Etsy Payments. UK VAT at 20% is added to Etsy's own fees. In total, expect to lose roughly 12–15% of a sale to Etsy fees before your own costs, and more if the sale is attributed to an offsite ad.

Does Etsy charge VAT on fees to UK sellers?

Yes. Etsy adds UK VAT (20%) to its service fees — including the transaction fee and payment processing fee. If you're not VAT-registered, this is a real cost you cannot reclaim. VAT-registered sellers can reclaim it through their normal VAT return.

How do I calculate my profit on an Etsy sale?

Start with your sale price, subtract the listing fee, transaction fee plus VAT, payment processing fee plus VAT, and any offsite ads fee if applicable. Then subtract your own costs: materials, packaging, postage, and a fair hourly rate for your time. What's left is your real profit. A spreadsheet with formula columns makes this much faster to repeat across different price points.
