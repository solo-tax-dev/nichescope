Etsy Returns and Refund Policy for Sellers: A Plain Guide

If you sell on Etsy, you need a returns and refund policy — not just because Etsy expects it, but because a clear policy protects you as much as it protects your buyer. This guide covers what Etsy actually requires, what you can decide for yourself, and how to write something that handles the awkward situations before they arise.

What Etsy Actually Requires From Sellers

Etsy does not force you to accept returns. You can set your shop to no returns, no exchanges, no refunds. But there are things you cannot opt out of:

  • Etsy's Purchase Protection programme means that if an item arrives damaged, doesn't arrive at all, or is significantly different from the listing description, a buyer can open a case. Etsy can then refund the buyer and, in some situations, cover that cost themselves — but only if you meet their seller standards and the listing was accurate.
  • Consumer law in the buyer's country still applies. UK buyers shopping from UK sellers, for example, have statutory rights under the Consumer Rights Act regardless of what your shop policy says. Your policy cannot override the law.

So 'no returns' in your shop settings means you won't voluntarily accept change-of-mind returns — it does not mean you are immune to cases or legal obligations.

The Difference Between Returns, Refunds, and Exchanges

These three things overlap but are not identical, and Etsy lets you configure each one separately in your shop settings.

Returns

A return means the buyer sends the item back and gets their money back. You can choose to accept returns within a time window you define (14 days, 30 days, 60 days) or not at all. If you accept returns, you can also decide who pays return postage — you or the buyer.

Refunds

A refund can happen without a physical return — for example, if something arrives broken and the buyer sends you a photo, you might refund without needing the item back. Refunds can also be partial: if a personalised item has a minor flaw, you might offer a percentage back rather than a full refund.

Exchanges

Etsy lets you indicate whether you accept exchanges. In practice, an exchange usually means issuing a refund and asking the buyer to place a new order — Etsy has no built-in swap mechanism. It is worth noting in your policy exactly how you handle this so buyers know what to expect.

Writing Your Shop Returns Policy

Your policy sits in your shop settings under the 'Policies' tab. Whatever you write there appears on every listing page. The clearest policies tend to do a few specific things:

  • State plainly whether you accept returns and over what timeframe.
  • Specify who pays return postage.
  • Explain what you do when something arrives damaged — most sellers ask for a photo within a set number of days.
  • Address personalised or custom items separately, because these are usually non-returnable and buyers should know that before they order.
  • Mention digital downloads separately if you sell them — these are almost always non-refundable because the file is delivered instantly.

Plain language works better than legal-sounding text. 'If your order arrives damaged, message me within 7 days and send a photo — I'll sort it' is more useful to a buyer than a paragraph of conditionals.

Personalised and Custom Orders

Personalised items are a common grey area. Because a custom item was made specifically for one buyer, many sellers do not accept returns on them — and that is generally reasonable. Etsy's own policies acknowledge that personalised items can be marked as non-returnable.

The risk is that buyers sometimes claim an item is 'not as described' when what they mean is 'I don't like how it turned out.' The best protection here is clear communication before you make anything: confirm the exact spelling, colours, size, and any other details in a message, and keep that message thread as your record.

When a Buyer Opens a Case

If a buyer is unhappy and opens an Etsy case rather than messaging you first, try not to panic. Here is the usual sequence:

  • Etsy gives you a chance to resolve the issue directly with the buyer before they step in.
  • If you cannot resolve it, Etsy reviews the case and can issue a refund to the buyer. Depending on the circumstances and your seller standing, they may or may not recoup that from you.
  • Cases count against your shop's Star Seller metrics and can affect your standing, so resolving them quickly and professionally matters.

The most common outcomes sellers report: offering a replacement or partial refund resolves most cases before Etsy has to intervene. A calm, prompt response goes a long way.

Refunds for Non-Delivery

If a buyer says their order never arrived, your options depend on whether you have tracking. With tracking, you can point to the carrier's records. Without it, you are largely relying on goodwill and your own judgment about whether the claim seems genuine.

For UK sellers sending internationally, it is worth weighing the cost of tracked postage against the risk of non-delivery claims, especially for higher-value items. Some sellers build the cost of occasional refunds into their pricing rather than pay for tracked shipping on every low-value order — neither approach is wrong, but it is a deliberate choice worth making consciously.

How Your Returns Policy Fits Into Pricing and Competition Research

Your refund policy is not just a customer service document — it is part of your product's total cost. If you offer free returns, that comes out of your margin. If you absorb the cost of reshipping damaged items, that affects your pricing too.

When you are researching a niche before you enter it, it is worth looking at what competitors are charging and what their policies look like. A seller offering free returns at a similar price to yours is either absorbing that cost or making it up somewhere else. A Few Practical Things Sellers Often Overlook

  • Update your policy when you change what you sell. If you add digital products or start doing custom work, your existing policy may not cover them properly.
  • Keep records of every resolution. If a buyer later escalates or leaves a review, you want to be able to show what you offered and when.
  • Refunds through Etsy, not PayPal or bank transfer. Processing refunds through Etsy's system keeps the transaction record clean and protects you if a case is ever reviewed.
  • Be consistent. If you refund one buyer in a situation, and then refuse another buyer in an identical situation, the inconsistency looks bad if Etsy ever reviews a case.

Where to Find and Update Your Policy

In Etsy's seller dashboard, go to Shop Manager, then Settings, then Policies. You will see separate fields for returns and exchanges, privacy, and custom FAQs. Changes take effect immediately and apply to all future orders. They do not retroactively change the terms of orders already placed.

It is worth reviewing your policy every few months, especially if Etsy updates its own seller policies — which it does periodically. Etsy sends emails about policy changes, but these are easy to skim past.

FAQ: Etsy Returns and Refund Policy for Sellers

Do I have to accept returns on Etsy?

No. Etsy lets you set your shop to accept no returns, no exchanges, and no refunds. However, this does not override consumer law in the buyer's country, and it does not prevent buyers from opening a case if an item arrives damaged or significantly different from the listing. Having a 'no returns' policy mainly means you are not obliged to accept change-of-mind returns.

Who pays for return postage on Etsy?

You decide. In your shop policies, you can specify that the buyer pays return postage, that you pay it, or that it depends on the reason for the return. Most sellers who accept returns require the buyer to cover postage for change-of-mind returns, and cover it themselves when the fault lies with the item or the seller.

Can I refuse a refund if my policy says no refunds?

For change-of-mind returns, generally yes. But if an item arrives damaged, is lost in the post, or does not match the listing description, Etsy's Purchase Protection and consumer law in many countries give buyers recourse regardless of your stated policy. A 'no refunds' policy is not a blanket protection against all claims.

How do I issue a refund on Etsy?

Go to Shop Manager, then Orders and Shipping, find the relevant order, and select 'Issue a Refund.' You can refund the full amount or a partial amount, and add a message to the buyer explaining the reason. Always process refunds through Etsy's system rather than outside it — it keeps the record clean and protects you if the transaction is ever reviewed.
