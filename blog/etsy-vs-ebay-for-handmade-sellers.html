Etsy vs eBay for Handmade Sellers: An Honest Comparison

If you make things by hand and you're trying to decide where to sell them, you'll almost certainly end up weighing up Etsy and eBay. Both are huge, both are accessible, and both charge fees. But they work very differently, and the right answer depends on what you make, how you price it, and how much competition you're willing to navigate.

This guide goes through the real differences — audience, fees, competition, and how to research any platform before you commit to listing on it.

The Fundamental Difference Between the Two Platforms

Etsy is built around handmade, vintage and craft supplies. Buyers come to Etsy specifically looking for something made by a person, often something personalised or unique. That intent matters. A buyer searching on Etsy for a hand-stamped silver necklace is not the same buyer searching eBay for a necklace.

eBay is a general marketplace. It started as an auction site, grew into a mass retail channel, and now competes directly with Amazon for everyday goods. Handmade items exist on eBay, but they're not the reason most buyers visit. You're competing on a platform where the default assumption is that the lowest price wins.

That one difference shapes almost everything else in this comparison.

Who Is Actually Buying on Each Platform?

Etsy buyers tend to be looking for something they can't find in a shop — a gift with a name on it, a piece of art that fits a specific aesthetic, something with a story behind it. They're often willing to pay more for that, and they expect to be buying from an individual maker rather than a warehouse.

eBay buyers are more varied. Many are looking for second-hand bargains, discontinued products, or the cheapest version of something widely available. There is a subset of eBay buyers who seek out handmade goods, but they're harder to reach because the platform's search and category structure isn't designed around handmade as a concept.

For most handmade sellers, this means Etsy delivers a more naturally aligned audience. That said, some product types do work on eBay — particularly handmade items that are functional, competitively priced, and easy to describe plainly (handmade candles, handmade soap, craft kits).

Fees: What You Actually Pay

Fee structures change, so always verify current rates directly on each platform. But here's how they broadly compare at time of writing.

Etsy fees

  • A listing fee per item, charged whether or not it sells.
  • A transaction fee on the sale price including postage.
  • Payment processing fees if you use Etsy Payments (mandatory in most countries).
  • Optional Etsy Ads spend on top of that.

UK sellers should also be aware that Etsy charges VAT on its seller fees. There's a useful breakdown of what you actually keep per sale on the NicheScope fees calculator article if you want to model your margins before listing.

eBay fees

  • A final value fee on the total transaction (item price plus postage).
  • Optional promoted listings fees if you use their advertising.
  • A small number of free listings per month, then insertion fees beyond that.

eBay's final value fees are competitive with Etsy's combined fees in some categories, but the comparison depends heavily on what category your item falls into. Neither platform is obviously cheaper across the board — it depends on price point, category and whether you run ads.

Competition: How Different Are the Two Landscapes?

On Etsy, competition is intense in popular handmade categories. Search for personalised gifts, wedding stationery or resin jewellery and you'll see pages of results. Many of those results are from sellers who've optimised heavily for Etsy SEO. Standing out takes real effort.

On eBay, the competition in handmade categories often looks thinner on the surface — fewer listings, less obvious optimisation. But that's partly because the buyer pool for handmade on eBay is also smaller. Fewer sellers doesn't automatically mean easier sales.

Before listing anywhere, it's worth doing actual research on the search results page rather than making assumptions. How many listings are there? How many reviews do the top sellers have? How are things priced? Are there lots of ads taking up the visible space?

Search and Discovery: How Buyers Find You

Etsy has its own search algorithm, and getting found on it is a discipline in itself. Titles, tags, attributes, recency and conversion rate all play a role. There's a reason entire tools exist just to help sellers with Etsy SEO.

eBay also has search (called Cassini), and it works on similar principles — relevance, seller performance, price competitiveness. But eBay also sends a lot of traffic through Google Shopping, which can be an advantage for items with clear search demand outside the platform.

Etsy does appear in Google results too, but the traffic pattern is different. Etsy tends to rank well for category and gift-type searches; eBay tends to rank well for specific product searches.

Brand Building and Repeat Customers

Etsy gives sellers a shop front, an About section, the ability to message customers, and a reviews system tied to your shop identity. Over time, a well-run Etsy shop builds a recognisable presence on the platform. Repeat buyers can favourite your shop and come back directly.

eBay has seller feedback and shop pages too, but the culture of repeat buying from a specific seller is less embedded. Buyers tend to transact and move on. Building a loyal following on eBay as a handmade seller is harder.

If brand building matters to you — and for most handmade businesses it eventually does — Etsy has more infrastructure for it.

Should You Sell on Both?

There's no rule against listing on both platforms simultaneously. Many sellers do. The practical challenge is inventory management — if you make one-of-a-kind items, you risk selling the same piece on both platforms at once and having to cancel an order. For made-to-order or produced-in-quantity items, that risk is lower.

If you're just starting out, trying to optimise two marketplaces at once splits your attention. Most sellers find it more useful to get one platform working before adding another.

When eBay Might Actually Make More Sense

There are genuine situations where eBay suits a handmade seller better than Etsy:

  • Your product is functional rather than decorative, and buyers comparison-shop on price.
  • You're selling in a category that's heavily saturated on Etsy but thinner on eBay.
  • You already have an eBay seller account with feedback history, which gives you a trust advantage.
  • Your items appeal to a general audience rather than a craft or gift-buying one.

eBay isn't the wrong answer by default — it's just the less obvious fit for most handmade goods. Worth testing if the product type fits.

How to Research Either Platform Before You Commit

Whatever platform you're considering, do the search yourself before listing. Go to the site, search your product keyword, and look at what comes up. Ask yourself:

  • How many results are there?
  • What are the top sellers charging?
  • How many reviews do established listings have?
  • How much of the results page is ads versus organic listings?

The Short Version

For most people making things by hand, Etsy is the more natural starting point. The audience is there specifically to buy handmade, the platform supports seller branding, and there's an established culture of paying a fair price for something made by a person. eBay is worth considering for certain product types or as an additional channel, but it's harder to build a handmade business there from scratch.

Whichever platform you choose, research the real competitive landscape before you invest time and money in stock. Numbers you can actually see on the page are more useful than estimates.

FAQ: Etsy vs eBay for Handmade Sellers

Is Etsy better than eBay for selling handmade items?

For most handmade sellers, yes. Etsy's audience comes specifically looking for handmade and unique goods, which makes it easier to attract buyers who understand and accept handmade pricing. eBay's audience is broader and more price-driven, which can make it harder to compete unless your product is also functional and competitively priced.

Can I sell handmade items on eBay?

Yes, eBay allows handmade listings. There's no dedicated handmade category the way Etsy has, but you can list under relevant product categories. Some handmade sellers do find success on eBay, particularly with functional items like candles, soap or craft supplies, where buyers are searching by product type rather than by the fact that something is handmade.

Are Etsy fees higher than eBay fees?

It depends on the category and sale price. Etsy's combined fees (listing, transaction and payment processing) can add up, especially for lower-priced items. eBay's final value fees vary by category. The honest answer is that you need to model your specific product at your specific price point on both platforms to know which is more expensive for you. Neither is categorically cheaper across the board.

How do I check competition on Etsy before I start selling?

The most straightforward method is to search your target keyword on Etsy and read the results page directly. Look at how many listings appear, what the top sellers are charging, how many reviews they have, and how many ad spots are visible.
