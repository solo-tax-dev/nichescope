How Many Photos Does an Etsy Listing Need?

Etsy allows up to ten photos per listing. Most experienced sellers use all ten slots. Whether you should do the same depends on your product — but there are clear reasons why more photos tend to help, and a handful of situations where a smaller, tighter set works just as well.

This article covers the practical side: what Etsy actually lets you do, what buyers expect to see, how to decide how many photos your specific product needs, and how to check what your competitors are already doing.

What Etsy Actually Allows

As of 2025, each Etsy listing supports up to ten photos and one video. Photos can be JPG, PNG, or GIF format. Etsy recommends a minimum of 2000 pixels on the shortest side for the best display quality across devices, particularly when buyers zoom in.

The first photo is the most important — it is the thumbnail that appears in search results and is the single image most buyers see before clicking. Everything else comes after that first impression.

Why Most Sellers Fill All Ten Slots

There is no penalty for using fewer photos, but there are real reasons why sellers who use more tend to convert better.

Buyers cannot touch your product

In a physical shop, a customer picks something up, feels the weight, reads the label, and inspects the stitching. Online, photos do all of that work. Every unanswered question a buyer has is a reason not to purchase. More photos reduce questions.

Different photos serve different buyers

Some buyers want to see the whole item. Others want close-ups of material or texture. Others want to see scale — how big is this, really? A lifestyle image helps them picture it in their home or on their body. A flat lay with dimensions answers size questions without needing to read the description. Each photo is working for a different type of buyer.

The video slot is separate

Adding a video does not replace a photo slot. It sits alongside your ten images. If you can record even a short clip showing the product from multiple angles, or demonstrating how it works, it is worth adding — it costs you nothing in the photo count.

How Many Photos Does Each Product Type Actually Need?

The honest answer is that it varies. Here is a realistic breakdown by category:

Physical handmade goods

Jewellery, ceramics, clothing, and similar items generally benefit from the full ten. You need a hero shot, multiple angles, at least one lifestyle image, a scale reference, and a close-up of any detail that justifies the price. Ten photos fills naturally if you think it through.

Digital downloads

A printable or digital template cannot be photographed in the traditional sense. Sellers here typically show mockups of the file in use — a print hanging on a wall, a planner page being written in. Five to seven strong mockups often does more than ten cluttered ones. Quality matters more than quantity.

Vintage items

Vintage listings benefit from thorough documentation. Buyers expect to see any wear, marks, or imperfections — not because they necessarily mind them, but because transparency builds trust and prevents returns. Ten photos is rarely too many for vintage.

Simple or small accessories

A single-colour enamel pin or a basic sticker might not have ten distinct things worth photographing. Forcing ten mediocre images is worse than using six good ones. Fill what you can fill well.

What Your First Photo Needs to Do

Because the first image is your thumbnail in search, it carries disproportionate weight. A few things worth knowing:

  • Square or slightly portrait crops work best. Etsy displays thumbnails in a square format; images that are very wide get cropped in ways you may not intend.
  • Clean backgrounds often outperform busy lifestyle shots in search. Once a buyer clicks, lifestyle images can close the sale — but in the grid, clarity wins.
  • Strong contrast and good lighting matter more than expensive equipment. A phone photo in natural light will outperform a studio shot with flat, grey lighting.

Common Photo Mistakes That Cost Sales

These come up repeatedly and are worth avoiding:

  • Blurry or low-resolution images. Buyers zoom. If the image pixelates, it signals low quality regardless of what you are selling.
  • No scale reference. 'How big is this?' is one of the most common reasons buyers abandon a listing. A hand, a coin, a ruler, or a labelled dimension on the image solves this instantly.
  • Only one angle. A single hero shot leaves buyers guessing about the sides, back, and interior of your product.
  • Misleading lighting or colour. If your product looks one colour on screen and arrives in a different shade, you will get returns and bad reviews. Accurate colour representation is worth the effort.
  • Skipping the lifestyle shot entirely. Abstract products especially — art prints, homeware, textiles — sell better when buyers can see them in context.

The Role of Alt Text and Photo Order

Etsy allows you to add alt text to each photo. This helps with accessibility and contributes to how Etsy and Google understand what the image contains. Write brief, descriptive alt text for each image — describe what is actually in the photo rather than keyword-stuffing.

Photo order also matters beyond the first image. Etsy uses your second and third images in certain placements and advertising formats. Keep your strongest images near the front of the sequence, not buried at the end.

A Practical Checklist Before You Publish

Before you go live, run through this quickly:

  • Is the first photo clean, well-lit, and square-friendly?
  • Do you have at least one lifestyle or in-context image?
  • Is there a clear size or scale reference?
  • Are close-ups included for any detail that justifies the price?
  • Have you shown the back, sides, or interior where relevant?
  • Is the colour accurate in natural or neutral light?
  • Have you added alt text to each image?
  • Have you added a short video if it adds value?

If you can check all of these, you are in good shape regardless of whether you end up using six photos or ten.

FAQ: How Many Photos Does an Etsy Listing Need?

What is the maximum number of photos you can add to an Etsy listing?

Etsy allows up to ten photos per listing, plus one video. The video slot is separate and does not count against your photo allowance. Most experienced sellers aim to use all ten photo slots where the product genuinely warrants it.

Do more photos on an Etsy listing help with SEO?

Etsy's ranking algorithm considers a range of factors, and listing completeness — including using your photo slots — is generally viewed positively. More importantly, more photos tend to increase conversion rates, and a listing that converts well signals quality to Etsy's search system over time.

What size should Etsy listing photos be?

Etsy recommends a minimum of 2000 pixels on the shortest side. This ensures photos look sharp when buyers zoom in. The ideal aspect ratio for thumbnails is square (1:1), though Etsy also supports portrait and landscape orientations. Avoid very wide or very narrow crops, as these can get awkwardly cropped in search grids.

How do I know how many photos my competitors are using?

The simplest way is to search for your target keyword on Etsy, click the top-ranking listings, and count their photos manually. Note the types of images they include — lifestyle shots, infographics, scale references — and look for patterns. This takes a few minutes and gives you real, visible data rather than estimates.
