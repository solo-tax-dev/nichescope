How Much Does eRank Cost? eRank Pricing Explained for Etsy Sellers

If you are weighing up eRank as part of your Etsy research toolkit, the first question is usually simple: what does it cost, and what do you actually get for that money? This article gives you a straight answer, then helps you decide whether a paid plan makes sense for where you are in your Etsy journey.

eRank Free vs Paid: The Basic Structure

eRank operates on a freemium model. There is a free tier, and there are two paid tiers. At the time of writing, the paid plans are typically called Basic and Pro, priced at roughly $5.99 and $9.99 per month respectively when billed monthly. eRank does adjust its pricing from time to time, so always confirm the current figures on eRank's own pricing page before committing.

The free plan is genuinely usable. You can run keyword searches, see trend data, and get a broad sense of competition in a niche. The catch is daily limits. Free users can only run a certain number of keyword lookups per day, and some of the more detailed reports are either locked or throttled. If you are doing occasional research, the free tier may be enough. If you are researching products every day or managing a large shop, you will hit the ceiling quickly.

What the Paid Plans Unlock

Basic Plan

The Basic plan roughly doubles or triples your daily keyword search allowance compared to the free tier. You also get access to more detailed competitor data, bulk keyword tools, and some additional listing audit features. For a seller who is actively researching new products or monitoring their keyword rankings, this is the tier most people find themselves on.

Pro Plan

The Pro plan is aimed at sellers with a large catalogue or those running multiple shops. It increases limits further and adds features like trend comparisons across longer time windows. Unless you are managing a high-volume shop or doing research at scale, most sellers find the Basic plan sufficient if they are going to pay at all.

What eRank's Data Actually Is

This is worth understanding clearly before you spend anything. eRank pulls keyword data from sources including Google Trends and Etsy's own search suggestions. It then models estimated search volumes and competition scores. These are useful signals, but they are estimates — Etsy does not publish actual search volume data to third-party tools, so no tool can show you a verified number for how many people searched a given term last month.

eRank is transparent about this in its own documentation, which is to its credit. The estimated figures help you compare keywords relative to each other rather than giving you a precise count. Treat them as directional rather than definitive.

The same principle applies to the sales and revenue estimates you will find in some Etsy research tools. Etsy does not share per-listing sales data with external platforms. Any tool that shows you an estimated monthly revenue figure for a competitor listing is working from a model, not from Etsy's actual data.

Is eRank Worth Paying For?

It depends on what you need.

If your main question is keyword research — which search terms to include in your titles and tags — eRank's paid tiers give you a reasonable increase in daily capacity and some useful comparative data. Many Etsy sellers find the Basic plan worthwhile if they are actively optimising listings or launching new products regularly.

If your main question is competition research — how saturated is this niche, what are competitors charging, how many reviews do the top listings have — eRank is less obviously the right tool. Its competition data relies on the same estimates that other tools use. You may find it more useful to look directly at what is actually on Etsy's search results pages: the prices, the review counts, the number of listings, and how many results are ads versus organic.

Common Reasons Sellers Consider Upgrading eRank

  • Hitting daily search limits: The free tier caps how many keywords you can look up each day. Active researchers hit this fast.
  • Wanting trend comparisons: Seeing whether interest in a keyword is rising or falling over a longer window.
  • Listing audits at scale: Checking multiple listings for SEO issues in one session rather than one at a time.
  • Competitor monitoring: Tracking how rival shops change their tags and titles over time.

None of these are trivial reasons to pay. They are also reasons to be clear-eyed about what you are getting: directional keyword intelligence, not verified sales data.

How eRank Compares to Other Paid Tools

eRank sits at the lower end of the price range for Etsy research tools. Tools like EverBee and Alura typically cost more per month and place heavier emphasis on estimated revenue figures for individual listings. EtsyHunt offers a similar freemium structure to eRank. Marmalead focuses specifically on keyword research rather than broader shop analytics.

All of these tools share the same underlying limitation: none of them have access to Etsy's internal sales data. The differences between them are mostly about interface, the breadth of features, and how aggressively they present estimated numbers as actionable.

If budget is a constraint, it is worth being deliberate about which one problem you most need to solve. Paying for multiple tools simultaneously is rarely necessary for a seller at the research or early-growth stage.

A Practical Approach to Etsy Research on a Tight Budget

If you are validating a new product idea or checking whether a niche is worth entering, you do not necessarily need a paid tool at all. Here is a workflow that costs nothing:

  • Search your target keyword on Etsy and note how many results appear.
  • Look at the first two pages and count how many listings are ads versus organic results. A high ad density often signals a commercially competitive niche.
  • Note the price range across the top listings and where the outliers sit.
  • Look at review counts on the top listings — a rough proxy for how established the leading sellers are.
  • Check whether Etsy's own search suggestions expand into related terms, which can indicate demand breadth.

FAQ: eRank Pricing and Etsy Research Tools

How much does eRank cost per month?

eRank currently offers a free plan and two paid tiers. Paid plans have been priced at approximately $5.99 per month for Basic and $9.99 per month for Pro, though eRank adjusts its pricing periodically. Check eRank's own site for the current figures before subscribing.

Is eRank's free plan actually useful?

Yes, within limits. The free plan lets you run keyword searches and see trend data, but daily lookup limits mean it becomes restrictive if you are doing regular, high-volume research. For occasional use or early-stage validation, many sellers find the free tier sufficient.

Does eRank show real sales numbers?

No — and neither does any other third-party Etsy tool. Etsy does not share per-listing sales data externally. The figures eRank and similar tools display are modelled estimates based on available signals. They can indicate relative popularity but should not be treated as precise sales counts.
