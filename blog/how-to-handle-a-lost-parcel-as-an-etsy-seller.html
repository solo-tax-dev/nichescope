How to Handle a Lost Parcel as an Etsy Seller

A parcel going missing is one of the more stressful things that can happen to an Etsy seller. You have done everything right — packed the order carefully, posted it on time, sent the tracking number — and then nothing. The buyer messages you, and you have to figure out what to do next without much guidance from Etsy itself.

This guide walks through the practical steps: what to check first, when to raise a claim, how to communicate with the buyer, and how to protect yourself going forward. No filler, just what actually matters.

Step One: Check Whether It Is Actually Lost

Before doing anything else, look at the tracking information carefully. A parcel is not necessarily lost just because it has not moved in a few days. Common reasons for apparent stalls include:

  • Customs delays for international orders — these can add weeks with no tracking updates
  • A scan being missed at a sorting depot
  • The parcel being held at a local delivery office because nobody was in
  • Public holidays in either the sending or receiving country

Ask the buyer to check with their local post office or delivery depot before you escalate anything. Many parcels that look lost turn up at a collection point the buyer did not know about.

As a rough guide, most carriers consider a parcel lost after a set number of business days beyond the expected delivery window — typically around 10 working days for domestic UK shipments and longer for international. Check the specific terms for the carrier you used, because this affects when you can raise a formal claim.

Step Two: Open a Claim With the Carrier

If the parcel is genuinely overdue and not at a collection point, you need to raise a missing parcel claim with the carrier. This is your route to compensation, and it has deadlines — so do not leave it too long.

For Royal Mail, you can usually claim for a lost item after it has been missing for a set number of working days beyond the expected delivery date. The claim window also has a closing deadline, typically several months from the date of posting, but check the current Royal Mail guidance because these thresholds do change.

What you will need to make a claim:

  • Proof of postage (the receipt from the post office or your carrier's despatch confirmation)
  • Proof of the item's value — an invoice, your Etsy order details, or a product listing screenshot
  • The tracking number
  • The buyer's address as you sent it

Keep all of this on file for every order. If you are printing labels at home, save a digital copy of each label alongside the order reference. It takes seconds and saves a lot of frustration later.

Compensation limits vary by carrier and the service you used. Basic services often cover only a small amount. If you are regularly shipping higher-value items, consider whether adding postal insurance or upgrading to a tracked and signed service makes financial sense.

Step Three: Talk to the Buyer Honestly

Buyers are usually far more understanding when they are kept informed. Silence is what causes tension. Once you have noticed a problem or received a message from the buyer, respond promptly and clearly.

A straightforward message might look something like this:

What to say: Acknowledge the delay, share the tracking information you have, let them know you are looking into it, and give a realistic timescale for when you will update them again. Do not make promises you cannot keep, and do not disappear for a week.

If the parcel was sent without tracking, be upfront about that. You have less information to offer, which is frustrating, but pretending otherwise will make things worse.

What Etsy Expects From You as a Seller

Etsy's Purchase Protection programme covers buyers in certain situations, including items that do not arrive. If a buyer opens a case through Etsy and you are found to be at fault — for example, because you sent to the wrong address or did not ship at all — Etsy can refund the buyer and charge you. If you shipped correctly to the address on the order, Etsy's policy is generally more protective of sellers, but the specifics depend on the case details.

The safest position is to ship to the address Etsy gives you, use tracked postage where possible, and keep proof of postage for everything. If you did all of that and the parcel is still missing, you are in a much stronger position when dealing with both the buyer and Etsy.

If a buyer opens a case, respond within the timeframe Etsy gives you. Ignoring a case is the worst outcome — it almost always results in an automatic ruling against the seller.

Should You Refund or Resend?

This depends on the situation, your margin, and what the buyer actually wants.

If you make or source the item yourself and have stock, resending is often the goodwill move that turns a frustrating experience into a positive review. If the item is bespoke or high-value, a refund may be more appropriate while you wait for the carrier claim to resolve.

Some sellers refund promptly and then recover costs through the carrier claim afterwards. Others wait for the claim to be resolved before deciding. Neither approach is universally right — it depends on your cash flow, the buyer's patience, and whether you think the parcel might still arrive.

Whatever you do, document your decision and communicate it to the buyer. A message saying exactly what you are doing and when they can expect resolution keeps the buyer calm and creates a clear record if a case is opened later.

How to Reduce the Risk Going Forward

Lost parcels cannot be eliminated entirely, but there are practical things that reduce both the likelihood and the damage when they happen:

  • Use tracked postage — it gives you and the buyer visibility, and it gives you evidence for a claim
  • Print labels rather than handwriting addresses — handwritten addresses are a common cause of misdelivery
  • Double-check the address before shipping — buyers occasionally enter addresses incorrectly; if something looks wrong, message before you post
  • Keep proof of postage for everything — even low-value items, because the habit matters
  • Consider postal insurance for higher-value orders — the cost is usually small relative to the protection it gives

Pricing your products correctly is also part of this picture. If your margins are so thin that one lost parcel creates a real problem, that is worth looking at separately. A Note on International Orders

International parcels go missing more often than domestic ones, and the claims process is more complicated. You are dealing with two postal systems, customs, and sometimes very long transit times that are technically normal.

If you ship internationally, it is worth being clear in your shop policies about estimated delivery times and the fact that you cannot control customs delays. Setting realistic expectations upfront means buyers are less likely to panic when a parcel takes three weeks to arrive.

For very remote destinations or countries with unreliable postal infrastructure, consider whether the risk is worth it, or whether to require tracked shipping as a minimum for those orders.

FAQ: Lost Parcels on Etsy

Who is responsible when an Etsy parcel goes missing — the seller or the buyer?

In most cases, the seller is responsible for ensuring the item arrives. If you shipped to the correct address with proof of postage and the parcel was lost in transit, you can pursue a claim with the carrier — but the buyer will generally expect you to resolve the situation regardless of what the carrier does. Etsy's Purchase Protection policy also places responsibility on sellers to ship correctly and on time.

How long should I wait before declaring a parcel lost?

This depends on the carrier and the destination. For domestic UK orders sent via Royal Mail, a parcel is generally considered lost after a set number of working days beyond the expected delivery date — check Royal Mail's current guidance as these thresholds change. For international orders, wait considerably longer, especially if customs is involved. Do not raise a formal claim too early, but do communicate with the buyer throughout.

Does Etsy refund sellers when a parcel is lost?

Etsy does not automatically refund sellers for lost parcels. If a buyer opens a case and Etsy rules in the buyer's favour, you may be required to issue a refund. If you qualify under Etsy's seller protection criteria — which generally requires shipping to the Etsy-provided address with proof of postage — Etsy may cover the refund in certain circumstances. Check Etsy's current Purchase Protection policy for the exact conditions, as these have changed over time.

Can I claim compensation from Royal Mail for a lost Etsy parcel?

Yes, if you used a Royal Mail service that includes compensation and you have proof of postage and proof of value. The amount you can claim depends on the service used — some cover only a small amount, while services like Special Delivery cover higher values. Submit your claim within the deadline Royal Mail specifies; leaving it too long means you lose the right to claim.
