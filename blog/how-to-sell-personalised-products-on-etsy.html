How to Sell Personalised Products on Etsy: A Practical Guide

Personalised products are one of the most searched categories on Etsy. Buyers love them because they feel special; sellers love them because a custom name or date can justify a higher price. But that popularity cuts both ways. Some personalised niches are genuinely strong opportunities. Others are so saturated that a new listing will never surface, no matter how good the product is.

This guide covers how to think through the decision properly — from picking a niche to understanding what the competition actually looks like — before you invest time, materials or money in making anything.

What Counts as a Personalised Product on Etsy?

Etsy uses the term broadly. A personalised product is anything a buyer can customise at the point of purchase — typically by adding a name, date, message, photo, or choosing a colour or size. Common examples include:

  • Custom name jewellery (necklaces, bracelets, rings)
  • Personalised home decor (signs, prints, cushions)
  • Wedding and anniversary gifts (keepsakes, cards, favours)
  • Baby and new parent gifts (milestone prints, christening items)
  • Pet portrait illustrations or personalised pet accessories
  • Custom clothing and accessories (embroidered items, printed tees)
  • Personalised stationery and planners

The fact that a product is personalised does not automatically make it a good niche. You still need to assess whether the market has room for another seller, and whether buyers are willing to pay enough to make the work worthwhile.

Why Personalised Products Can Be Harder Than They Look

Because personalisation adds perceived value, lots of sellers pursue it. That means many of the obvious categories — custom name necklaces, family name signs, birth stat prints — have very large numbers of existing listings, many from established shops with hundreds or thousands of reviews.

Reviews matter because Etsy surfaces listings that convert well, and buyers trust shops with social proof. A new seller entering a crowded niche starts with zero reviews, which makes ranking difficult regardless of listing quality.

There is also the fulfilment side. Each personalised order requires you to read the buyer's instructions carefully, produce the variation correctly, check it, and deliver it on time. Mistakes on personalised orders are harder to fix — a misspelled name on a physical product can mean a replacement at your cost. Building your processes to handle this reliably is essential before you scale.

How to Assess a Personalised Niche Before You Commit

The most practical first step is to search for the product you are thinking of making, exactly as a buyer would, and look at what comes up. You want to understand three things from that search results page: how many listings exist, what prices are being charged, and how much of the page is taken up by paid adverts.

Listing count and review density

A high listing count is not automatically disqualifying, but it is a signal. More importantly, look at the review counts on the listings that appear in the first two rows. If the top results all have several hundred or several thousand reviews, you are looking at a well-established competition set. If there are listings with strong placement but modest review counts, there may be more room to compete.

Price distribution

Scroll through the first page and note the price range. Are most sellers charging similar amounts, or is there a wide spread? If the majority of listings are priced well below what you need to charge to cover your costs and time, that is a red flag before you have made a single item.

Ad density

Etsy labels sponsored listings. Count how many of the visible results are ads versus organic. A page dominated by ads tells you that established sellers are paying to stay visible — which often means organic ranking is harder to achieve in that niche, and that new entrants may need to advertise to get any traction at all.

Finding a Workable Angle in a Crowded Category

Most of the broad personalised product categories are crowded. The opportunity usually sits one or two levels deeper — in a more specific combination of product, style, occasion or audience.

Instead of searching for personalised print, try searching for the specific occasion or recipient alongside the product. Personalised retirement gift teacher or custom name puzzle toddler are far more targeted. When you search these narrower phrases, you will often find fewer listings and sometimes weaker competition — lower review counts on the top results, fewer ads, less price compression.

This is not a guarantee of success. It is a signal worth investigating. Run several searches around a product idea to see whether the pattern holds across different phrasings, or whether one narrow phrase just happens to have low competition because no one is actually searching for it.

Pricing Personalised Products Correctly

Personalised products often attract a premium, but sellers frequently undercharge because they anchor their price to mass-produced alternatives rather than the actual cost of their own time and materials.

A useful exercise is to look at the prices on the first page of results for your target niche and note where the cluster sits. Then work out your real costs — materials, Etsy fees, packaging, any platform or tool costs — and what hourly rate you want to earn for the production time. If your honest price sits well above what most listings are showing, you either need a clearly differentiated product that justifies it, or you need to reconsider the niche.

If your honest price sits comfortably within the range shown on the first page, that is a reasonable signal that the market can support your pricing. It is not a guarantee of sales, but it removes one major obstacle.

Setting Up Your Listings for Personalised Products

Once you have validated a niche and set a realistic price, the listing itself needs some specific attention for personalised products.

The personalisation field

Etsy has a dedicated personalisation field you can enable on any listing. Use it, and write clear instructions in it — tell buyers exactly what information to provide, in what format, and flag anything that might cause confusion (for example, whether they should include punctuation, or how they should format a date). Vague instructions lead to mistakes and unhappy buyers.

Photos that show the personalisation

Show the product with a real example of personalisation in place — not just a blank product. Buyers want to see what their finished item could look like. If you can show a range of name lengths or styles, that helps too. Mockup images are widely used, but lifestyle photos — showing the item in context — tend to convert better.

Turnaround time

Be honest about how long personalised orders take. Buyers choosing a personalised product for a gift need to know it will arrive in time. If your processing time is longer than average in your niche, consider whether you can tighten it — or whether you should target buyers with more lead time (for example, positioning items as gifts to order in advance rather than last-minute purchases).

Before You Scale, Test

A common mistake is to invest heavily in supplies, equipment or print-on-demand setup before validating that a specific personalised product will sell. Start with a small batch or a handful of listings. See what search terms bring visitors to those listings (Etsy's own analytics will show you this). Adjust based on what you observe, not what you assumed.

FAQ: Selling Personalised Products on Etsy

Do personalised products sell better than non-personalised ones on Etsy?

Not automatically. Personalised products can command higher prices and often have strong gift-purchase intent, which helps conversion. But many personalised niches are also very crowded, which makes it harder for new listings to rank. The answer depends almost entirely on which specific product and niche you are in.

Can I use print on demand for personalised Etsy products?

Yes. Several print-on-demand platforms support personalisation — they allow buyers to submit names or text at the point of ordering, and the platform prints and ships the item. Etsy permits print on demand provided the shop is transparent about it. The competition in print-on-demand-friendly niches (such as custom name prints or personalised mugs) tends to be high, so niche selection matters even more.

How do I handle mistakes on personalised orders?

If the mistake is yours — you misread the buyer's instructions — you should remake and reship at your own cost. If the mistake is the buyer's — they submitted incorrect information — the situation is less clear-cut, but most experienced sellers find that replacing the item anyway and updating their listing instructions to prevent the same issue is better for reviews and repeat business than disputing responsibility. Clear instructions upfront are the best prevention.

Is it worth entering a saturated personalised niche as a new seller?

It is harder, but not impossible. The key is to enter with a genuinely differentiated product — different style, different material, different target occasion — rather than trying to compete directly with established shops on the same search terms. Use real search data to find sub-niches within the broader category where competition is lighter, and price honestly from the start rather than undercutting and burning yourself out.
