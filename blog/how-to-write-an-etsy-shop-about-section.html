How to Write an Etsy Shop About Section That Actually Works

Your Etsy shop about section is one of the few places on Etsy where you can speak directly to a potential buyer without competing with listings, ads, or search results. Most sellers either skip it entirely or fill it with vague sentences that say nothing useful. This guide will help you write one that actually does a job.

Why the About Section Matters

Buyers who visit your about section have already shown interest. They clicked through from a listing, liked what they saw, and wanted to know more before committing. That is a warm audience. If the page is empty or reads like it was written in two minutes, you lose an opportunity that cost you nothing to take.

Etsy also uses your shop content as part of how it understands what your shop is about. A well-written about section, with clear language about what you make and who it is for, helps Etsy surface your shop in the right places. It is not a replacement for good listing SEO, but it supports it.

What to Actually Put in Your About Section

Etsy gives you up to 5,000 characters. You do not need to use all of them. What you do need is to answer the questions a real buyer would have.

Who makes the things in your shop

Etsy buyers often choose it specifically because they want something made by a person, not a warehouse. Tell them who you are. Not a life story, but enough to establish that there is a real maker behind the work. Where you are based, how long you have been doing this, what drew you to it. Keep it brief and honest.

What you make and how you make it

Be specific about your materials, your process, or your tools if that is relevant to what makes your products worth buying. If you hand-stamp every piece of jewellery, say that. If your prints are made using original watercolour paintings, say that. Specifics build trust in a way that phrases like 'made with love' never will.

Who your shop is for

You do not have to spell this out as a bullet point, but the best about sections make a buyer feel like they are in the right place. If you make personalised gifts for new parents, the tone and detail of your about section should reflect that. If you sell mid-century modern ceramics to people who take their interiors seriously, it should feel like that too.

Any practical details worth knowing

If you offer custom orders, mention it. If you have been selling for several years and have completed thousands of orders, that is a credibility signal worth including. If everything is made to order and takes a set amount of time, a sentence about that sets expectations early and reduces queries later.

What to Avoid

A few patterns come up again and again in about sections that do not work.

  • Vague opening lines. Sentences like 'Welcome to my shop, where I pour my heart into everything I create' tell the buyer nothing. Start with something concrete.
  • Repeating your shop title back to them. They can already see it. Use the space to add information.
  • Making it entirely about you. Your background matters, but the buyer wants to know what is in it for them. Balance your story with how it connects to what you sell.
  • Leaving it blank. Even a few short paragraphs are better than nothing. An empty about section suggests either a new shop with no track record or a seller who is not paying attention.
  • Keyword stuffing. Writing naturally is better than forcing search terms into every sentence. Your about section is not a listing title.

How to Structure It

There is no single correct structure, but a pattern that works well for most shops looks like this:

  • One or two sentences establishing who you are and what you make.
  • A short paragraph about your process, materials, or what makes your work different.
  • A sentence or two about who you make things for or what occasions or needs your shop serves.
  • A brief closing that mentions custom orders, response times, or anything else useful.

You can write this as flowing paragraphs or break it into short sections. Either works. What matters is that someone reading it in thirty seconds comes away with a clear picture of your shop.

Using the Video and Photo Features

Etsy lets you add a video and up to five photos to your about section. If you have footage of yourself making something, or photos of your workspace, these are worth adding. They reinforce the handmade or small-business nature of your shop in a way that words alone cannot. A short process video, even filmed on a phone, can do more to build trust than several paragraphs of text.

Refreshing Your About Section Over Time

If your shop has changed since you wrote your about section, update it. If you have expanded your range, improved your process, or hit a milestone like a certain number of completed orders, these are worth reflecting. Buyers notice when a shop feels current and active.

It is also worth revisiting your about section when you are researching how your shop compares to others in your niche. A Note on Tone

Write in the same way you would talk to a customer who emailed you. Plain, clear, warm without being over the top. Etsy buyers tend to be sceptical of shops that sound like marketing copy. If you read your about section back and it sounds like an advert, rewrite it as if you were explaining your shop to a friend.

Avoid superlatives like 'the best', 'unique', or 'one of a kind' unless you can back them up with something specific. 'Every piece is hand-stitched using traditional embroidery techniques I learned from my grandmother' is more convincing than 'my items are truly one of a kind.'

Checking Your Work Before You Publish

Before you save your about section, ask yourself three questions. Does it tell a buyer who makes the products? Does it explain what makes them worth buying? Does it give any useful practical information? If you can answer yes to all three, you are in good shape. If not, find the gap and fill it.

Etsy Shop About Section FAQ

Does the Etsy about section affect search rankings?

Etsy has not confirmed exactly how shop content influences search, but using clear, relevant language in your about section helps Etsy understand what your shop sells. It is not a substitute for strong listing titles and tags, but it supports your overall shop presence and helps buyers who land on the page decide whether to stay.

How long should an Etsy about section be?

There is no ideal length, but something in the range of two to four short paragraphs is usually enough to cover the essentials without losing the reader. Etsy allows up to 5,000 characters. Most effective about sections use a fraction of that. Clarity matters more than length.

Can I use my about section to promote a sale or discount?

You can, but it is better used for evergreen content about your shop and process. Promotional details go out of date and make the page feel neglected when they are no longer accurate. If you want to promote a sale, Etsy has other tools for that, including coupons and announcements.

What if I sell in multiple categories — how do I write an about section that covers everything?

Focus on what connects the different parts of your shop rather than trying to list every product type. If you sell hand-dyed yarn and knitting patterns, for example, both come from a love of fibre crafts. Lead with that common thread and let the listings do the work of showing the range.
