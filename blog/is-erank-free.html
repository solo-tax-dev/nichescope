Is eRank Free? What Etsy Sellers Actually Get

If you're researching Etsy tools and wondering whether eRank costs anything, the short answer is: there is a free plan, but it comes with meaningful limits. This article walks through exactly what you get, what you don't, and how to fill in the gaps without spending money.

Yes, eRank Has a Free Plan

eRank offers a free tier that gives you access to a range of features without entering payment details. You can sign up with an email address and start using the tool straight away. That's genuinely useful if you're just starting out and don't want to commit to a subscription before you know whether the tool fits your workflow.

The free plan includes access to keyword research tools, trend data, and some listing audit features. For a seller who's just testing the waters, that's a reasonable starting point.

Where the Free Plan Falls Short

The limitations on the free tier are where things get more nuanced. eRank caps how many searches or lookups you can do per day on the free plan. If you're doing serious niche research — comparing multiple product categories, checking dozens of keywords, or analysing competitor listings in depth — you'll hit those limits quickly.

Some of the more detailed reports and competitor tracking features are also restricted to paid tiers. The free plan gives you a taste, but sustained research tends to require an upgrade.

What the Paid Plans Add

eRank's paid plans (Basic and Pro, at the time of writing) unlock higher daily search limits, more detailed keyword data, bulk listing analysis, and additional competitor insights. If you're running an active shop and using eRank as your primary research tool, the paid tiers are probably necessary.

Whether that's worth the monthly cost depends entirely on how often you're doing research. For sellers who dip in occasionally, the free plan might stretch far enough. For those researching new products regularly, it likely won't.

What Kind of Data Does eRank Show?

This is worth understanding clearly, because it affects how much trust to place in the numbers you see.

eRank, like most Etsy research tools, shows estimated figures for things like monthly searches and competition scores. These are modelled estimates — eRank doesn't have access to Etsy's internal search data, and Etsy doesn't publish it. The estimates are built from crawled data and statistical modelling, which means they can be a useful guide but aren't guaranteed to reflect what's actually happening on the platform.

This isn't a criticism unique to eRank. EverBee, Alura, and similar tools all work with estimated data for the metrics Etsy doesn't expose. The important thing is knowing that when you see a 'monthly searches' figure, it's a model output, not a number pulled directly from Etsy's systems.

What Data Is Actually Reliable on Etsy?

There's a category of Etsy data that's completely reliable because it's right there on the page: prices, review counts, the number of results a search returns, and which listings are showing as ads. These figures are visible to anyone browsing Etsy and don't require any estimation.

This is the data that actually answers the most practical questions:

  • What are sellers in this niche charging?
  • How many reviews does a typical top listing have — and is that achievable?
  • How many competing listings exist for this search term?
  • Is this search results page dominated by paid ads, which would make organic visibility harder?

None of that requires a subscription or an estimated model. It's sitting on the page. The only friction is that pulling it together manually takes time.

Who Should Stick With eRank's Free Plan?

The free plan is a reasonable fit if:

  • You're in the early stages of setting up a shop and doing light research
  • You only need to look up a handful of keywords per week
  • You want to get familiar with the tool before deciding whether to pay

It's probably not enough if:

  • You're validating multiple product ideas in a short period
  • You're an existing seller doing regular competitor and pricing research
  • You're hitting the daily search cap before you've finished your research session
