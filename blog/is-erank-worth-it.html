Is eRank Worth It? An Honest Look for Etsy Sellers

If you have spent any time researching Etsy tools, eRank almost certainly came up. It is one of the longest-standing Etsy keyword and market research tools available, and it has a loyal following. But the question sellers keep asking is a fair one: is eRank actually worth paying for, or can you get what you need without a subscription?

This article gives you a straight answer — covering what eRank does well, where its limitations are, what the free tier actually includes, and when a different approach to Etsy research might serve you better.

What eRank Actually Does

eRank is primarily a keyword research tool for Etsy. It helps you find search terms people use on Etsy, shows how competitive those terms are, and lets you analyse listings — your own and competitors'. It also includes a listing audit feature that checks your tags, titles, and descriptions against SEO best practices.

The free plan is genuinely usable. It gives you a set number of keyword lookups per day, access to trend data, and some listing analysis. The paid tiers increase your daily lookup limits and unlock additional features like bulk keyword analysis and more detailed competitor data.

Where the Estimates Come From

This is the part that matters most and gets talked about least.

Etsy does not publish sales data. It does not release revenue figures for individual shops or listings. Any tool that shows you an estimated monthly sales number or estimated revenue is working from a model — typically built on review counts, listing age, and observed patterns. Those estimates can be useful as a rough directional signal, but they are not the same as actual sales data.

eRank is upfront that its sales estimates are modelled figures, not official numbers from Etsy. The same applies to other popular tools in this space, including EverBee and Alura. None of them have access to data Etsy does not share publicly. Their estimates reflect their methodology, not a data feed from Etsy itself.

This is not a criticism unique to eRank — it is simply how the whole category of tools works. Understanding that distinction helps you use any of these tools more sensibly.

What eRank Does Well

Keyword research is where eRank earns its reputation. Its database of Etsy search terms is substantial, and the trend graphs help you see whether interest in a term is growing, flat, or declining. If you are trying to find less competitive long-tail keywords for your listings, eRank is a legitimate tool for that job.

The listing audit is also genuinely helpful for sellers who are new to Etsy SEO. It flags obvious gaps — missing tags, titles that are too short, categories that do not match — in a format that is easy to act on.

For sellers who are already on Etsy and focused on optimising existing listings, the keyword features alone may justify the cost of a paid plan, depending on how often you are adding or updating listings.

Where eRank Falls Short

If your goal is to validate a product idea before you make it — before you invest in materials, photography, or a prototype — estimated sales numbers are a shaky foundation to build on. You are being shown a figure that represents a model of what might be happening, not confirmed purchase data.

Similarly, if you are researching pricing, estimated revenue figures can mislead you. A listing that appears to be generating significant monthly revenue based on an estimate might simply have a high review count because it has been running for years, or because the seller actively encouraged reviews. The estimate does not distinguish between those scenarios.

The free plan also has daily lookup limits that can feel restrictive if you are doing serious research across multiple niches in a single session.

The Alternative: Research What Is Actually Visible

There is a different way to approach Etsy competition research — one that sticks to what is actually on the page rather than working from modelled estimates.

When you search for a product on Etsy, the results page contains real, verifiable information: the prices sellers are charging, the number of reviews on each listing, how many of the results are paid adverts versus organic listings, and how many total results exist for that search. None of that requires estimation. It is simply what Etsy shows anyone who looks.

That visible data tells you quite a lot. A niche where most of the top results have very few reviews suggests the competition has not been around long, or has not built much traction — which may be an opportunity. A niche where every top result shows thousands of reviews and the first row is mostly ads tells a different story. You can read both situations accurately from the page itself, without needing a modelled sales figure.

So Is eRank Worth It?

It depends what you are trying to do.

If you are an active Etsy seller who regularly creates new listings and wants a structured keyword research workflow, eRank offers real value. The keyword database is solid, the trend data is useful, and the listing audit saves time. Whether the paid plan is worth it over the free plan depends mostly on how many keyword searches you need per day.

If you are primarily trying to validate whether a product idea is worth pursuing — or researching what price point a market will support — the estimated sales and revenue figures across all these tools deserve some healthy scepticism. Treat them as one signal among several, not as confirmed data.

And if your main question is simply: is this niche too crowded, and what are people charging? — that is a question you can answer with what is already visible on the Etsy results page, for free, right now.

Practical Checklist: Getting the Most From Etsy Research Tools

  • Use keyword tools for what they are good at: finding search terms, spotting trends, and auditing listing SEO. eRank does this competently.
  • Treat estimated sales figures as directional, not definitive: use them to compare listings relative to each other, not as absolute revenue data.
  • Cross-reference with what is actually on the page: check review counts, pricing ranges, and ad density in real search results to ground your research in visible facts.
  • Do not pay for features you will not use: if you only need occasional keyword lookups, the free tier of most tools is sufficient to start.
  • Validate ideas before you invest: the most useful research happens before you make the product, not after you have already listed it.

FAQ

Is eRank free to use?

Yes, eRank has a free plan that includes a limited number of keyword lookups per day and access to core features. Paid plans increase those limits and unlock additional functionality. The free tier is a reasonable starting point to assess whether the tool suits your workflow before committing.

How accurate are eRank sales estimates?

eRank, like other Etsy market research tools, generates its sales estimates using modelled data — typically based on review counts, listing age, and observed patterns. Etsy does not publish individual listing or shop sales figures. The estimates can give you a rough sense of relative activity, but they should not be treated as precise sales data.

What is the difference between eRank and EverBee?

Both tools offer Etsy keyword research and estimated market data. EverBee places more emphasis on revenue estimates and product analytics; eRank has a stronger focus on keyword research and listing SEO auditing. Both work from modelled estimates rather than published Etsy sales data. Which is more useful depends on what stage of research you are in and what questions you are trying to answer.

Can I do useful Etsy research without a paid tool?

Yes. Etsy search results themselves contain real, verifiable data — prices, review counts, number of results, proportion of paid adverts — that can tell you a great deal about a niche without needing any subscription.
