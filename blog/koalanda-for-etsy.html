Koalanda for Etsy: What It Does and What to Consider

If you've been searching for ways to research Etsy niches and competitors, you've probably come across Koalanda. It sits alongside a crowded field of Etsy research tools — EverBee, eRank, Alura, Sale Samurai — all promising to help you make smarter decisions about what to sell and how to price it.

This article takes a plain look at what Koalanda actually does, where its limits are, and what your alternatives are — including a free one that works differently from all of them.

What Is Koalanda?

Koalanda is a web-based Etsy research tool aimed at sellers who want to analyse market demand, spot trends, and size up the competition before listing a product. It offers a keyword explorer, product research views, and shop analysis features — broadly similar to what you'd find in other tools in this category.

The pitch is familiar: type in a niche or product idea, and get data back that's supposed to tell you whether there's demand, how much revenue top sellers are making, and whether the niche is worth entering.

How Koalanda Works

Like most Etsy research tools, Koalanda doesn't have access to Etsy's internal sales data. Etsy doesn't publish actual sales figures publicly — the platform shows review counts and a few surface-level signals, but real transaction volumes are private.

What tools like Koalanda do instead is build estimated sales figures using algorithms. They combine visible signals — review counts, listing age, search position — with proprietary modelling to produce numbers that look like sales data. The figures can be useful as a rough directional guide, but they are estimates, not facts. Koalanda is transparent enough about this in its methodology, but it's worth being clear in your own mind about what you're buying.

If you see a listing described as making a certain number of sales per month, that number is a model output — not something Koalanda or any other tool has actually observed happening.

What Koalanda Shows

Koalanda's core features typically include:

  • Keyword research: Search volume estimates and related keyword suggestions for Etsy searches
  • Product research: Listings ranked by estimated performance, with estimated monthly sales and revenue attached
  • Shop analysis: A breakdown of a competitor's shop including estimated revenue, top listings, and tag usage
  • Trend data: Directional signals about whether a niche is growing or declining

These features are broadly useful for getting a starting framework when you're exploring a new product idea. The risk is treating the estimated numbers as precise — they're not, and they can vary considerably from what's actually happening.

The Limits Worth Knowing

No criticism of Koalanda specifically here — these limits apply to the whole category of Etsy tools that use estimated data:

Estimates compound uncertainty

When a tool estimates sales from review counts, it has to assume a conversion rate between purchases and reviews. That rate isn't published by Etsy and varies by niche, price point, and listing age. If the assumed rate is off, every downstream figure — revenue, market size, seller ranking — is off too. You can end up with figures that look precise but carry significant hidden uncertainty.

Keyword search volumes are estimates too

Etsy doesn't publish keyword search volume data. Any figure labelled as search volume in any third-party tool — Koalanda included — is modelled from available signals, not read directly from Etsy's systems. That doesn't make it useless, but it means you shouldn't treat a difference of a few hundred searches between two keywords as meaningful.

Pricing research can be done without estimates

One thing you can always verify yourself: what competitors are actually charging. Real prices are right on the page. Same for review counts and how many listings are competing in a search. You don't need estimated revenue to understand a market's price range or how saturated it looks.

What Koalanda Costs

Koalanda operates on a subscription model with a free tier that offers limited access and paid plans unlocking more searches and features. Pricing has changed over time and varies by region, so check the current Koalanda pricing page for the latest figures rather than relying on any number quoted here.

Whether it's worth the subscription depends on how frequently you're doing product research and whether you're comfortable basing decisions on modelled data. For sellers who are validating ideas regularly or managing a large catalogue, having a centralised research tool can save time even with the estimation caveats. For occasional research, the cost-benefit is harder to justify.

Koalanda vs Other Tools: A Quick Comparison

Koalanda sits in roughly the same category as EverBee, Alura, and Sale Samurai — all showing estimated sales and revenue. eRank takes a slightly different angle, focusing more on keyword data and listing optimisation scores, though it also uses estimated figures where Etsy data is unavailable.

The meaningful distinction isn't really between these tools — it's between tools that estimate and tools that only report what's verifiable. Both approaches have legitimate uses. The key is knowing which type you're using and adjusting how much weight you put on the numbers accordingly.

How to Use Koalanda Well (If You Choose It)

If you do use Koalanda, a few practical habits help:

  • Use estimated sales directionally, not absolutely. A listing estimated at several hundred sales a month is probably doing better than one estimated at ten. Whether the exact number is accurate matters less than the relative signal.
  • Cross-check prices manually. Real prices are always worth verifying on the actual Etsy search page. Tools can lag on price changes or show incorrect figures if a listing has been updated.
  • Look at review counts alongside estimates. Review counts are real and visible. If a listing has five reviews and a tool claims it's making hundreds of sales a month, treat that with scepticism.
  • Pay attention to ad density. How many of the first search results are sponsored listings tells you how competitive the paid advertising is in that niche — something estimated revenue figures don't capture.

FAQ

Is Koalanda free?

Koalanda offers a limited free tier, but meaningful access to its research features requires a paid subscription. Exact pricing varies and is best checked directly on their site.

Does Koalanda show real Etsy sales data?

No. Like all third-party Etsy tools, Koalanda does not have access to Etsy's internal sales figures. The sales and revenue numbers it displays are estimates produced by its own modelling, not data Etsy has published or shared.

Is Koalanda accurate?

It depends on what you mean by accurate. The estimates are based on real signals and thoughtful modelling, so they can be directionally useful. But they can also be significantly off for individual listings — especially newer listings, very high-priced items, or niches where the review-to-purchase ratio differs from the tool's assumptions. Treat them as rough indicators rather than hard figures.
