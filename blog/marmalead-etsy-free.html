Marmalead Etsy Free: What You Get and What to Use Instead

If you've been searching for a free way to research Etsy, Marmalead probably came up. It's one of the longer-standing Etsy keyword tools, and sellers often ask whether the free version is enough to be useful. This article gives you a straight answer — and then looks at what your actual options are if you want solid research without paying a monthly fee.

What Marmalead Actually Is

Marmalead is a keyword and SEO tool built specifically for Etsy sellers. Its main job is to help you find search terms that shoppers are using, assess how competitive those terms are, and optimise your listing titles and tags accordingly. It's been around long enough to build a genuine following, and plenty of sellers credit it with improving their search visibility.

The tool pulls in data related to Etsy search behaviour and uses it to score keywords, suggest alternatives, and flag whether a term is trending or declining. It also shows estimated engagement metrics to help you gauge demand.

Is Marmalead Free?

Marmalead does have a free tier, but it's limited in a meaningful way. You can create an account and explore the interface, but the free access cuts off before you reach the features that most sellers actually need. In practice, you can get a feel for how the tool works, but you can't do thorough keyword research or pull full data on a niche without upgrading to the paid plan.

The paid plan is billed monthly and sits at a price point that's reasonable if you're selling consistently, but harder to justify if you're still in the 'should I even make this product?' stage. There's no free trial in the traditional sense — you get limited access, not a full-featured window that expires.

What the Free Version Lets You Do

Within the free tier you can generally browse keyword suggestions and see partial information. You won't get full keyword grades, storm search comparisons, or the deeper engagement data that makes the tool useful for real decisions. Think of it as a preview rather than a working research suite.

If you're an existing seller wanting to refine your SEO strategy and you're willing to pay, Marmalead makes sense to evaluate properly. If you're trying to validate a product idea before spending time on it, or you just want to understand the competitive landscape without committing to a subscription, the free version won't take you far enough.

The Bigger Question: What Kind of Data Do You Actually Need?

This is worth stepping back on, because the type of research you're doing changes which tools are genuinely useful.

Keyword research vs competition research

Marmalead is primarily a keyword tool. It helps you understand search terms — which ones get traffic, which are oversaturated, which might be worth targeting. That's genuinely useful for writing listings and planning your SEO.

But there's a separate question that keyword data can't fully answer: what does the actual competition look like in search results right now? How many sellers are running ads? What price range are listings clustering in? How many reviews do the top results have? These are things you can only see by looking at the live search page itself.

Estimated figures vs real on-page data

Most Etsy research tools — including Marmalead, EverBee, eRank, and Alura — work with estimated figures at some point. Etsy doesn't publish sales data publicly, so when a tool shows you monthly sales or revenue estimates for a listing, those are modelled numbers, not figures Etsy has confirmed. The models can be reasonable, but they're still estimates, and sellers sometimes make decisions based on numbers that turn out to be quite different from reality.

There's a different approach: only look at what's verifiably on the page. Review counts are public. Prices are public. Whether a listing is an ad is visible. How many results a search returns is visible. None of that is estimated — it's just what Etsy is showing anyone who searches.

How the Two Tools Compare in Practice

These tools are doing different jobs, so comparing them directly is only partially useful. But here's a practical breakdown:

  • Marmalead free tier: limited keyword data, no full access without paying, best suited to SEO-focused sellers who want to invest in a keyword strategy
  • Marmalead paid: fuller keyword and engagement data, useful for listing optimisation, estimated figures for demand signals
  • NicheScope: real on-page competition data, fully free, no account, no estimates — best for understanding a niche before committing or checking where you sit relative to competitors

If you're already selling and want to improve your search rankings, a keyword tool like Marmalead has genuine value. If you're starting from scratch or evaluating a new product idea, having real pricing and competition data in front of you is often more immediately useful than keyword scores.

A Practical Workflow for Etsy Research Without Paying

Here's how you can approach product research using only free tools:

  • Search the niche on Etsy and note the result count — a useful rough signal for how many sellers are active in the space
  • Use NicheScope to see how many of the top results are ads, what the price spread looks like, and how many reviews the established listings have
  • Check Etsy's own search suggestions and related searches to understand how buyers are phrasing their queries
  • Look at the listings themselves — read the reviews, notice what buyers mention, and spot gaps in what's being offered
  • Use the free tier of a keyword tool like eRank or Marmalead to get a rough sense of search term competitiveness, understanding those figures are estimated

None of this costs money. It takes more manual effort than a paid all-in-one tool, but the data you're looking at for competition research is real rather than modelled.

The Bottom Line on Marmalead's Free Version

Marmalead's free tier exists more as a product demo than a working research tool. If you're serious about keyword-led SEO on Etsy and you're generating consistent revenue, it's worth evaluating the paid version properly. If you're not at that stage yet, or if what you really need is to understand the competitive landscape in a niche before investing time in it, the free version won't serve you well.

For competition research specifically, tools that show you real on-page data — prices, review counts, ad density — give you something more concrete to work with than estimated figures, particularly at the validation stage when the stakes of a wrong assumption are highest.

FAQ: Marmalead Etsy Free — What Sellers Actually Ask

Is Marmalead completely free to use?

No. Marmalead has a free tier that lets you explore the interface and see partial data, but full access to keyword grades, search comparisons, and engagement metrics requires a paid subscription. The free version is better described as a preview than a functional free plan.

What does Marmalead show that Etsy doesn't?

Marmalead surfaces keyword competitiveness scores, search engagement trends, and related term suggestions that aren't available in Etsy's own search interface. It also organises keyword data in ways that make it easier to compare terms when writing listing titles and tags. Some of the underlying figures — particularly around demand — are estimated rather than directly published by Etsy.

Are there genuinely free Etsy research tools that don't require a subscription?

Yes. NicheScope is one example — it's a Chrome extension that shows real competition data from Etsy search pages (prices, review counts, ad density) with no account and no paid tier. eRank also has a meaningful free plan for keyword research, though with daily usage limits. Neither replaces a full paid tool, but both give you useful data at no cost.

Should I use a keyword tool or a competition checker first?

It depends on where you are in the process. If you're still deciding whether a niche is worth entering, competition data — how many sellers are there, what are they charging, how established are the top results — is usually more immediately useful. Once you've decided to pursue a niche, keyword research helps you write listings that surface in search. Many sellers find it useful to do a quick competition check first, then layer in keyword research once the niche passes that initial filter.
