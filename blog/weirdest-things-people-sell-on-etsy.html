Weirdest Things People Sell on Etsy (And What Sellers Can Learn)

Etsy is genuinely one of the strangest marketplaces on the internet. That is not a criticism — it is what makes it interesting. Somewhere right now, somebody is listing a crocheted plague doctor mask, a hand-poured candle that smells like a library, or a cross-stitch pattern of a disgruntled badger. And somebody else is buying it.

This article is part curiosity trip and part practical guide. Because the weird corners of Etsy are not just entertaining — they actually teach you something useful about how niches form, how buyers find what they want, and how to research any product idea before committing to it.

Some of the Weirdest Things People Actually Sell on Etsy

These are all real categories that have established listings with genuine reviews — not one-off jokes.

Taxidermy and Oddities

A whole community of sellers trades in what collectors call 'oddities': preserved insects pinned in shadow boxes, animal skulls, Victorian mourning hair jewellery, and Victorian-era medical curiosities. Some of this is genuinely centuries old; some is newly made to look antique. It has consistent demand from collectors, gothic home decorators, and prop buyers for film and theatre. The listings often carry long, detailed descriptions and high prices — which tells you something about the buyer: knowledgeable, specific, not impulse-purchasing.

Personalised Pet Portraits in Bizarre Styles

Standard pet portraits are mainstream now. The weird end is where it gets interesting: your dog painted as a Tudor monarch, your cat rendered as a Soviet propaganda poster, your hamster depicted as a Renaissance saint. These are not novelty items that sell once — the category has accumulated serious review counts across multiple sellers. People give them as gifts, and they photograph well, which feeds the social sharing loop that brings more buyers.

Snail Mail Surprises and Mystery Boxes

A category of sellers sends you something unexpected in the post. You pay a fixed amount, you state a theme or mood, and something arrives. No product photos, no exact description of contents. This is genuinely unusual for e-commerce, where buyers normally expect to know what they are getting. It works because the uncertainty is the product — the experience of receiving something is what is being sold.

Hyperspecific Digital Downloads

This is where Etsy gets very niche very fast. Not 'printable planner' — more like 'printable weekly planner specifically designed for people who keep chickens' or 'moon phase tracker formatted for left-handed bullet journalists'. The specificity is deliberate. Broad digital products compete with thousands of listings; a narrow product competes with almost none, and if a buyer searches for exactly that thing, they find you immediately.

Scented Candles Themed Around Fictional Worlds

Candles that smell like specific rooms in a fantasy novel. Candles described as 'a rainy afternoon in a hobbit hole' or 'the inside of the TARDIS'. The product is partly physical and partly imaginative — buyers are paying for the association and the story as much as the wax and fragrance. This category requires almost no explanation to its target audience, which is a sign of a tight niche with a self-selecting buyer pool.

Crochet Patterns for Deeply Specific Things

Not crochet patterns in general — patterns for a crochet version of a specific vegetable, a particular species of frog, or a convincing miniature of an everyday object. A pattern for a crocheted butter dish. A pattern for a life-size crocheted cauliflower. These sell as digital downloads, cost a few pounds, and if the niche is specific enough, there may only be one or two sellers in the world offering that exact thing.

Handmade Miniatures

Miniature food, miniature furniture, miniature household objects so detailed they look real in close-up photographs. Some are for dolls houses; some are just for display. The craftsmanship required is considerable, which means pricing can be high, and buyers tend to be serious collectors rather than casual browsers. Review counts in this category often reflect repeat customers who buy across a seller's whole catalogue.

What These Niches Actually Have in Common

Look at the examples above and a pattern emerges. The weirdest things on Etsy are not weird to the people buying them. Every one of these categories has a defined audience that knows exactly what it wants, searches for specific terms, and — because the mainstream market does not serve them — turns to a handmade marketplace.

The other common thread: specificity. The sellers who do well in odd niches are not trying to serve everyone. They have gone narrow enough that competition thins out, and the buyers who do find them are already pre-qualified. A person searching for a crocheted cauliflower pattern is not price-shopping across forty alternatives. They want that specific thing.

How to Use Weird Niches as Research Inspiration

Even if you have no intention of selling taxidermy or fictional-world candles, browsing the strange corners of Etsy is a legitimate research technique. Here is why:

  • They show you what specificity looks like. If a crocheted cauliflower pattern has forty reviews and almost no competition, that tells you something about what happens when you go narrow enough.
  • They reveal buyer psychology. People buy stories, associations, and identity expressions — not just objects. A candle that smells like a fictional library is selling a feeling.
  • They demonstrate that review counts matter more than category size. A niche with few listings and steady reviews is often healthier for a new seller than a huge category with thousands of established competitors.
  • They highlight the power of differentiation. In a category with a dozen listings, being the best-photographed or best-described option is achievable. In a category with ten thousand listings, that is much harder.

Checking Competition in Any Niche, Weird or Not

The same research approach applies whether you are considering a hyperspecific oddity or a more mainstream product like a personalised birthday card. Search for your intended keyword on Etsy, look at the results, and ask:

  • How many listings appear?
  • What are the prices at the low, middle, and high end?
  • How many reviews do the top listings have — and are those reviews recent?
  • How much of the first page is paid advertising versus organic results?

High ad density on a search results page usually means sellers are paying to appear because organic ranking is competitive. Low ad density can mean the opposite — or it can mean buyers are not searching for that term at all. Context matters.

A Note on Tools That Show Revenue Estimates

Several popular Etsy research tools — EverBee, eRank, Alura and others — show estimated monthly sales or revenue figures for listings. These numbers are model outputs, not data from Etsy, because Etsy does not share individual listing sales figures with third parties. The estimates may be useful as a rough directional signal, but they are not verified figures. If you are making a significant decision about what to make or stock, it is worth knowing whether the numbers you are looking at are measured or modelled.

That distinction matters most when you are validating a genuinely unusual product idea — exactly the kind of niche this article covers.

FAQ

What are the most unusual things people sell on Etsy?

Some of the more unusual categories include Victorian mourning hair jewellery, animal taxidermy and oddities, hyperspecific crochet patterns (including patterns for individual vegetables or animals), scented candles themed around fictional worlds, and mystery postal surprises where the buyer does not know what they will receive. All of these have real buyers and genuine review histories — they are not gimmicks.

Can you actually make money selling weird or niche things on Etsy?

Yes, and often more reliably than in crowded mainstream categories. A seller in a very specific niche with limited competition can rank organically without heavy advertising spend, and buyers who find a niche listing are often ready to buy. The challenge is that the total audience is smaller, so you need to price appropriately and may need to offer a range of related products to build volume over time.

How do I check if a weird niche on Etsy has enough competition to be worth entering?

Search for your target keyword on Etsy and look at the results page directly. Note how many listings appear, what the price range is, how many reviews the top listings have, and how many of the visible results are paid ads. A niche with few listings and sparse reviews suggests low competition but possibly low demand too. A niche with many listings, high review counts, and heavy ad presence suggests strong demand but established competition. Tools like NicheScope can read this data from the page and present it clearly, without requiring an account or showing guessed figures.

Does Etsy allow the sale of unusual or unconventional items?

Etsy has a specific policy requiring items to be handmade, vintage (at least twenty years old), or craft supplies. Unusual items are generally fine as long as they meet those criteria and do not fall into prohibited categories (which include certain animal products, hazardous materials, and items that violate other policies). Taxidermy and animal oddities, for example, are permitted with restrictions — sellers in that category are usually familiar with the specific rules. If you are considering an unusual product, it is worth checking Etsy's prohibited items list directly before investing time in it.
