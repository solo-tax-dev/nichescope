What Does Bestseller Mean on Etsy?

If you have spent any time browsing Etsy, you will have noticed the small orange Bestseller badge sitting on certain listings. It looks like a signal — and sellers researching a niche often treat it as one. But what does it actually mean, and how much weight should you give it when you are deciding whether to enter a market?

What the Bestseller Badge Actually Is

Etsy awards the Bestseller badge to individual listings, not to shops as a whole. According to Etsy, a listing earns the badge when it has been one of the most popular items in its category. Etsy does not publish a precise sales threshold, and the badge can appear and disappear over time as a listing's performance changes relative to others in the same category.

A few things worth noting straight away:

  • The badge is assigned by Etsy's algorithm — sellers cannot apply for it or buy it.
  • It is category-relative. A listing in a small niche can earn Bestseller with far fewer sales than one in a crowded category.
  • Etsy does not publicly state exactly how many sales trigger the badge, so any specific number you see quoted elsewhere is an estimate, not a fact.

What the Badge Does Not Tell You

This is where it gets important for research purposes. The Bestseller label tells you a listing has done well — it does not tell you how well, how recently, or why.

No sales count attached

Etsy does not display a sales figure next to the badge. You can see a listing's total review count on the page (a useful proxy, since most buyers do not leave reviews, but a higher count does suggest more sales). The badge itself, though, carries no number with it.

No timeframe

A listing could have earned the badge during a seasonal spike two years ago and still be displaying it now. Or it could be actively selling every week. From the badge alone, you cannot tell.

Category thresholds vary widely

A Bestseller badge in personalised wedding gifts means something very different from one on a niche printable planner template. The same badge can represent vastly different levels of demand depending on how competitive the surrounding category is.

How Sellers and Third-Party Tools Interpret It

Because Etsy does not publish sales data, a whole industry of third-party tools has grown up around estimating what the badge might imply. Tools like EverBee, eRank, and Alura use various modelling approaches to generate estimated monthly sales or revenue figures for listings that carry the Bestseller badge and others. These estimates can be useful as rough directional signals, but they are exactly that — estimates built on indirect signals, not figures Etsy has provided.

It is worth being clear-eyed about this. If a tool tells you a Bestseller listing is making a specific revenue figure per month, that number is modelled, not measured. Etsy does not share transaction-level data with third-party developers.

What You Can Actually See on the Page

There is a practical alternative to relying on estimated figures: look at what Etsy itself shows you directly on any search results page.

  • Review count — visible on each listing card. More reviews generally mean more sales over time, even if the ratio varies.
  • Price — tells you what the market is actually willing to pay, right now.
  • Bestseller badge presence — useful as a yes/no signal of relative popularity within the category.
  • Ad density — how many of the visible results are paid ads rather than organic listings. High ad density suggests strong commercial competition.
  • Number of results — shown at the top of Etsy search, indicating how many listings are competing for that query.

None of this requires a paid subscription. It is all sitting there on Etsy's own pages.

Using the Bestseller Badge Sensibly in Niche Research

The badge is most useful when you treat it as one data point among several, rather than as proof that a niche is profitable or that you should enter it.

Look at how many Bestseller listings appear in a search

Run a search for your target keyword on Etsy. How many of the first page results carry the badge? If nearly every listing does, the category is well-established and competition is likely strong. If very few do, the niche may be newer or smaller.

Cross-reference with review counts

A Bestseller badge plus a high review count (several hundred or more) suggests a listing has had sustained demand over time. A badge with a low review count might mean the listing is newer and growing, or that the category threshold is simply low.

Check the price range across Bestseller listings

If most Bestseller items in a niche sit in a narrow price band, that tells you what buyers in this category expect to pay. Pricing significantly above that band without a clear differentiation is usually difficult to sustain.

Look at ad density alongside the badge

If Bestseller listings are mostly organic (not ads) and review counts are strong, that suggests genuine buyer demand. If a search page is dominated by ads, some sellers may be paying to look popular rather than earning it organically.

A Realistic Picture of What Bestseller Signals

Here is a straightforward way to think about it. The Bestseller badge is Etsy saying: relative to other listings in this category, this one has sold well. That is a meaningful signal. It tells you there is real buyer interest, and it tells you the listing has managed to convert that interest into purchases.

What it does not do is give you a business case. For that, you need to understand the full competitive landscape: how many other listings exist, what prices the market supports, how many reviews your likely competitors have built up, and whether demand is organic or propped up by advertising spend.

Treating the badge as a starting point — not a conclusion — is the most practical way to use it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the Etsy Bestseller badge mean a listing is currently selling well?

Not necessarily. The badge reflects past performance relative to a category, and Etsy does not remove it immediately when sales slow down. A listing can retain the Bestseller label even if its sales have declined significantly from their peak. Review count trends are a more reliable indicator of whether demand is ongoing.

How many sales does it take to get the Bestseller badge on Etsy?

Etsy has not published a specific threshold. The badge is category-relative, which means the number of sales required in a small niche is likely much lower than in a large, competitive one. Any specific figures you see quoted online are estimates based on observation, not official data from Etsy.

Can a seller buy or fake the Bestseller badge?

No. The badge is assigned by Etsy's algorithm based on actual sales performance within a category. Sellers cannot pay for it, apply for it, or otherwise manufacture it. It is entirely system-generated.

Is the Bestseller badge useful for deciding whether to sell in a niche?

It is useful as one signal among several. The badge tells you that demand exists. It does not tell you how much competition you will face, what prices the market supports, or whether there is room for a new seller to gain traction. Looking at review counts, pricing across the first page of results, and ad density alongside the badge gives you a much more complete picture before you commit to a new product.
