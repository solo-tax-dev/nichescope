What Is Etsy Offsite Ads and Can You Opt Out?

If you have noticed an unexpected fee on an Etsy sale and spotted the label 'Offsite Ads' in your payment account, you are not alone. It catches a lot of sellers off guard the first time, and the way Etsy explains it in their help docs is not always easy to parse. This guide covers exactly what the programme is, how the fee is calculated, who can opt out, and what you should factor into your pricing if you are stuck in it permanently.

What Etsy Offsite Ads Actually Are

Etsy runs its own advertising across external platforms — Google Shopping, Facebook, Instagram, Pinterest, and Bing, among others. It pays for those placements itself, without any upfront cost to sellers. When a shopper clicks one of those ads and then buys from your shop within 30 days, Etsy charges you a percentage of that order as a commission for generating the sale.

The key point: you do not set the ads up, you do not choose where they appear, and you do not pay anything unless a sale actually results. The cost only appears after the conversion happens.

How the Offsite Ads Fee Is Calculated

Etsy charges either 12% or 15% of the order total (including postage, but before other Etsy fees), depending on how much your shop has made over the past 365 days.

  • 15% fee: shops that have made under £10,000 (or the local currency equivalent) in sales over the previous 12 months.
  • 12% fee: shops that have exceeded that threshold.

The fee applies to the full order value from that buyer within the 30-day attribution window, not just the item that was originally clicked. So if someone clicks an Offsite Ad for one of your prints and then adds three more items to their basket in the same session, the fee applies to the whole order.

It is worth stressing: this fee comes on top of Etsy's standard transaction fee (6.5%), your listing fee, and any payment processing fees. For lower-margin products, an Offsite Ads sale can end up noticeably less profitable than an organic one. That is exactly why your base pricing matters so much — if your margins are already tight, an unexpected 12–15% commission can tip a sale into a loss.

Who Can Opt Out of Etsy Offsite Ads?

This is where a lot of sellers feel frustrated, because the opt-out is not available to everyone.

Shops below the sales threshold

If your shop has made less than £10,000 in the past 365 days, you can opt out of Offsite Ads. Etsy enrols all eligible shops automatically, but smaller shops retain the choice to leave the programme. To do that, go to Shop Manager, then Settings, then Offsite Ads, and toggle the participation off.

Shops above the sales threshold

Once your shop crosses the £10,000 mark in a 12-month period, participation becomes mandatory. Etsy's position is that at that scale, Offsite Ads form a meaningful part of how your traffic is generated, and they lock you in accordingly. You cannot opt out, and there is no workaround available through normal account settings.

Some sellers feel strongly that this is unfair — particularly those running high-volume, low-margin shops where the fee has a real impact on profitability. That frustration is understandable. But as things stand, if you have crossed the threshold, the fee is part of your cost structure and needs to be built into your prices.

Is the Offsite Ads Fee Worth It?

That depends entirely on your margins and your product type. For sellers with healthy margins, an Offsite Ads sale is still a profitable sale — you just keep a smaller slice of it. For sellers running very close to break-even, every Offsite Ads commission can sting.

There is also an argument that for shops below the threshold who can opt out, staying in the programme can make sense. You are essentially getting advertising you did not pay for upfront, and you only pay if it converts. If your margins support it, that is a reasonable trade.

The counter-argument is that you have no visibility into where your listings are being shown, what copy Etsy is using for the ads, or how your brand is being positioned alongside competitors. For some sellers — particularly those with a carefully built brand identity — that lack of control matters.

How Offsite Ads Interact With Your Pricing Strategy

If you are in the mandatory programme, the practical implication is simple: your pricing needs to account for a potential 12% or 15% fee on any given sale, on top of all other Etsy costs. If you have not done that already, your effective margin on Offsite Ads sales may be lower than you realise.

A useful exercise: work out what you actually keep from a sale after transaction fees, payment processing, Offsite Ads at 15%, and your materials or production costs. If that number is negative or negligible, your prices need to go up.

This is also a good moment to look at what competitors are charging for similar products. Not to copy them blindly, but to understand whether there is room in the market to hold higher prices. What Shows in Your Payment Account

When an Offsite Ads fee is charged, it appears as a separate line item in your Etsy payment account, labelled clearly. It is deducted from your available balance in the same way other fees are. You will also see which order triggered it, though Etsy does not tell you which specific platform the ad appeared on.

If you want to track how often Offsite Ads are converting for you, you can export your payment account CSV and filter by the Offsite Ads fee type. This gives you a rough sense of what proportion of your sales are coming through the programme over time.

Common Misconceptions About Etsy Offsite Ads

Do you pay if someone clicks but does not buy?

No. You only pay if there is a completed purchase within 30 days of the click. Clicks alone cost you nothing.

Does the fee apply to every sale?

No. It only applies to sales that Etsy can attribute to an Offsite Ad click. Sales coming through organic Etsy search, direct links, or Etsy Ads (the on-platform advertising product) are not affected by Offsite Ads fees.

Is Offsite Ads the same as Etsy Ads?

No, and this trips people up regularly. Etsy Ads is the separate, optional, pay-per-click system that puts your listings higher in Etsy search results. You set a daily budget and pay for clicks. Offsite Ads is entirely different — it is Etsy advertising your listings externally, with fees only charged on resulting sales.

Etsy Offsite Ads FAQ

Can I see which platform my Offsite Ad appeared on?

No. Etsy does not disclose which external platform (Google, Facebook, Pinterest, etc.) generated the click that led to a sale. You can see that an Offsite Ads fee was charged and which order it relates to, but not the source platform.

Does the 30-day window reset if the buyer clicks another Offsite Ad?

Yes. The 30-day attribution window restarts with each new Offsite Ad click from the same buyer. If someone clicks an ad, does nothing, then clicks another Offsite Ad two weeks later and buys, the 30-day window runs from the second click.

What happens to my Offsite Ads status if my sales drop back below the threshold?

Once a shop has crossed the threshold and been made a mandatory participant, that status does not automatically reverse if sales dip below the threshold in a subsequent period. Etsy has not provided a clear public mechanism for reverting to optional status after passing the threshold.

Does Offsite Ads affect my Etsy search ranking?

No. Offsite Ads operates entirely outside Etsy's internal search algorithm. Participation or non-participation has no documented effect on where your listings appear in Etsy search results.
