The problem

The most expensive mistake on Etsy
happens before you list.

Hours of making, photographing and writing — sunk into a niche that was already decided. Here's what usually goes wrong:

a.

The niche looked open. It wasn't.

Page one was quietly stacked with ads and 1,000-review incumbents — invisible unless you count them by hand.

b.

The price was a guess.

Priced too high for the market, or raced to the bottom against sellers you never knew existed. (Before you price anything, it's worth knowing exactly what Etsy's fees take off the top.)

c.

The "research tool" made numbers up.

Etsy doesn't reveal sales data to anyone. Tools that claim to know it are estimating — and charging monthly for the estimate.

Fair questions

Before you ask

Is it really free?

Yes — everything described on this page is free, with no usage caps and no trial clock. If paid features arrive later, they'll be new additions on top; nothing free today gets taken away.

Why no sales estimates like other tools?

Because Etsy doesn't share sales data with anyone. Tools showing "monthly revenue" are modeling a guess — and those guesses are the most common accuracy complaint in their own reviews. NicheScope only reports what's verifiably on the page, clearly labeled as exactly that.

What data does it collect about me?

None. There's no account, no analytics beacon, no server. The analysis runs inside your browser on the page you're viewing, and the results stay on your device.

Does it work on small niches?

Yes — it analyzes whatever the search returns. Small niches simply mean a smaller sample (say 15–20 listings instead of 65), which is still plenty to read pricing and competition at a glance.
